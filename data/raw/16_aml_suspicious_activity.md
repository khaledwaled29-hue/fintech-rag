# AML and Suspicious Activity Reporting

Document ID: FP-CMP-016 | Owner: Compliance | Version 3.3 | Effective: 1 March 2026 | Classification: Internal

## Purpose
This policy describes how FinPay detects and reports suspected money laundering and terrorist financing.

## Red flags
- Rapid movement of funds in and out of an account with no clear purpose.
- Structuring: repeated transactions just below reporting thresholds.
- Transfers to high-risk jurisdictions without a clear business reason.
- Activity inconsistent with the customer's stated occupation or business profile.
- Use of many accounts linked to the same device or address.

## Escalation
Any employee who suspects money laundering must raise an internal alert to the Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) through the compliance case tool on the same day.

## Reporting
The MLRO decides whether to file a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) with the Financial Intelligence Unit. SARs must be filed within **30 days** of the initial detection.

## Large transaction reporting
Single transactions or linked transactions totalling **USD 10,000 or more** in one day are automatically reported for review.

## Tipping-off
Employees must **never** tell a customer that a SAR has been filed or that they are under investigation. Tipping-off is a criminal offence. If the customer asks why their account is restricted, support agents use the approved script: "Your account is under a routine review. We will contact you when it is complete."

## Training
All employees complete AML training at onboarding and annually.
